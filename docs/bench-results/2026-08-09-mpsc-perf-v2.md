# MPSC perf v2: shift adopted, padding rejected, Park measured

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Hardware:** 4-core Linux VM, 15 GiB RAM, no swap; box checked with `vmstat`
(83–90% idle, 0 runnable) rather than load average, which lags badly here
**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-07-mpsc-perf-v2-design.md` (revised scope)

Three levers, three different outcomes.

## 1. Shift instead of divide — ADOPTED, with no measurable speedup

`seq / cap` became `seq >> shift` on every publish and every consumer poll.
`cap` is a runtime field, so the compiler could not strength-reduce the
division; it executed as a hardware `div`.

`mpsc/busy_spin_2_producers`, cap 1024, 2 producers, six runs each:

| | runs (Melem/s) | mean |
|---|---|---:|
| before (divide) | 29.31, 30.19, 31.55 | ~30.4 |
| after (shift) | 30.35, 30.48, 31.22, 31.12, 30.65, 30.75 | **30.76** |

**No measurable change** — the delta sits inside the cell's ~2.9% run-to-run
spread. Kept anyway, per the spec's per-lever gate: removing a hardware
division from two hot paths at zero semantic risk is correct whether or not a
benchmark notices.

The negative result is itself informative. A `div` is roughly 20–40 cycles
against ~1 for a shift, on a path executed once per element — and it vanishes
into the noise. That says the MPSC hot path is dominated by cross-core traffic
(CAS retries, cache-line transfer), not by ALU work, and it is weak evidence
against any remaining hope that arithmetic micro-optimisation will move this
design.

## 2. Padded availability array — REJECTED

`avail: Box<[AtomicI64]>` → `Box<[CachePadded<AtomicI64>]>`, giving each round
entry its own 64-byte line instead of sharing one line between eight.

### First measurement (single cell) looked like a win

`mpsc/busy_spin_2_producers`, A-B-A-B blocked, six runs each:

| | mean | range |
|---|---:|---|
| unpadded | 30.76 | 30.35–31.22 |
| padded | 31.84 | 31.04–32.46 |

**+3.5%**, Welch's t ≈ 4.2, p < 0.01. It cleared the spec's gate.

### Second configuration killed it

Because padding trades false sharing for cache residency — benefit growing with
producer contention, cost growing with capacity — the result was re-tested
across three configurations (`mpsc_layout_probe`), six runs padded against
three unpadded, interleaved A-B-A:

| cell | unpadded | padded | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| cap1024_p2 | 30.41 | 31.02 | +2.0% |
| **cap4096_p2** | **32.28** | **32.25** | **−0.1%** |
| cap1024_p4 | 24.91 | 25.75 | +3.4% (spreads ~10%) |

**At cap 4096 padding buys nothing.** That is the cache-residency prediction
confirmed: unpadded `avail` at cap 1024 is 8 KiB and fits a typical 32–48 KiB
L1d; padded it is 64 KiB and does not; at cap 4096 padded it is 256 KiB, and
the residency cost cancels the false-sharing benefit exactly.

The cap-1024 gain also shrank from +3.5% to +2.0% merely by moving to the probe
harness, and the 4-producer cell has ~10% run-to-run spread that swamps any
effect. An improvement that halves when the harness changes and disappears when
the capacity changes is not a property of the design.

### Decision

Reverted. The cost was `cap × 64 B` — 64 KiB per channel at cap 1024, an 8×
blow-up on that array and ~4.5× on the whole channel (~16 KiB → ~72 KiB for a
`u64` payload). Not justified by a benefit that does not survive a change of
configuration. `design.md` §8's defence of the compact, cache-resident layout
stands, now with a measurement behind it rather than only an argument.

**This also weakens §8's attribution.** If false sharing on `avail` were a
dominant cost, eliminating it entirely would show more than +2.0% at the
configuration where it should help most. Combined with lever 1's null result,
neither of the two costs §7 and §8 name for the MPSC gap has been shown to
matter much — and the dominant cost remains unidentified.

## 3. Park-mode bake-off — MEASURED

New `bakeoff_park_mpsc` group. Every other MPSC cell drives `try_*` with a
hand-rolled spin, so neither crate's blocking path had ever been measured.
2 producers, cap 1024, blocking `send`/`recv` on both sides:

| Cell | Melem/s | range |
|---|---:|---|
| `ultima_park` | 15.89 | 15.53–16.14 |
| `crossbeam_blocking` | 50.46 | 49.89–51.02 |

**0.31× — crossbeam is ~3.2× faster on the blocking path.** The spec's original
gate wanted Park within ~1.5× of crossbeam; it is not close.

A plausible cause, not verified here: this crate's `Park` pays a `SeqCst` fence
plus a wake on *every* operation (design.md §8), while crossbeam parks only
when it actually blocks and spins first. Confirming that needs a profile, not
this number.

## Where this leaves the MPSC

Both semantics-preserving levers are now spent. One is kept on principle with
no measured gain; the other was measured and rejected. The batched claim — the
Disruptor-grade lever, and a protocol change with a real correctness surface —
remains the only untried option for the shared-claim design, and the decision
on it returns to the human.

For callers who can give up global FIFO, `src/sharded.rs` already answers the
throughput question at 4.51× crossbeam
(`docs/bench-results/2026-08-07-sharded-mpsc.md`).
